# Stance: explainer

**Purpose:** make a subject clear, memorable and properly sourced.
**Voice:** curious, precise and generous, without sounding academic or generic.
**Lead with it when** the video's main job is making a topic understood through narration, visual explanation, data, diagrams or examples.
**Default for:** `animated-explainer`, `avatar-spokesperson`.

## Priorities

1. Teaching clarity: one idea at a time, in an order where each step makes the next inevitable.
2. Factual accuracy: every claim traceable to a source in the research.
3. Visual explanation: show what is hard to say; a diagram that builds as it is named beats a paragraph.
4. Narration pacing: about 150 words a minute, with silence after the insight.
5. A memorable mental model: an analogy or picture the viewer keeps tomorrow.

## Questions to ask

- What must the audience understand by the end, in one sentence?
- Which misconception or unanswered question creates the hook?
- What can be shown more clearly than it can be said?
- Where is the "aha", and does the structure build toward it?

## Avoid

- Decorative visuals with no explanatory job.
- Generic listicle structure ("five things about…").
- Unsupported claims, invented numbers, vague sources.
- Dense narration with no visual breathing room.
- Exact text (names, numbers, calls to action) inside generated images.

## Quality bar

- The script's word count fits the duration within 10% at the planned pace, and the finished video lands within 5% of its target.
- Each narration section stays within about 15% of its planned time; when it runs long, the script is shortened rather than the pictures squeezed.
- Narration and pictures agree at every moment: each line starts with the visual it describes (within half a second).
- Every generated or stock image looks like part of the same video: palette and style anchors hold.
- Every number on screen matches the research; every source is cited in the delivery.
- Presenter-led pieces: the presenter stays primary, support graphics stay sparse, the spoken copy sounds natural, and the call to action lands once and clearly.
- Problems go back to the stage that can fix them: length to the script, sync to the edit, visuals to assets or the scene plan, audio to compose. Small timing drift is fixed downstream; only structural problems go back upstream.
