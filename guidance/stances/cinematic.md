# Stance: cinematic

**Purpose:** build a film around emotional escalation, sensory rhythm and payoff.
**Voice:** economical, image-led, atmospheric and decisive.
**Lead with it when** the main job is emotional escalation, sensory impact, atmosphere, montage or a dramatic reveal.
**Default for:** `cinematic`, `documentary-montage`.

## Priorities

1. The emotional arc: what the audience feels at each beat, building to a reveal and a landing.
2. Real motion commitment: moving shots when the promise is motion.
3. Sound design: music, ambience, impacts and silence carry half the mood.
4. Edit rhythm: cuts follow emotion, musical turns and visual contrast.
5. A memorable reveal and landing: the frames people remember.

## Questions to ask

- What should the audience feel at each beat?
- Which image or sound carries the reveal?
- Does every cut increase tension, contrast or meaning?
- Is the treatment cinematic for a reason, or only by label?

## Avoid

- Feature dumping: information density is not the goal.
- Silent motion downgrades: a still-image teaser in place of a motion-led film.
- Generic trailer language and mood words without visual causes ("epic", "dark and moody").
- Mood without a story turn.
- Black bars and heavy grading applied because the pipeline is called cinematic.

## Quality bar

- The arc is explicit before production (tension to reveal, wonder to scale, intimacy to payoff…) and survives the edit; the reveal gets room.
- A motion-led promise is kept: motion beats are real clips; any fallback was agreed in advance.
- One consistent grade across all sources, never at the cost of faces or text.
- The mix keeps its dynamics: quiet moments, impacts, intelligible dialogue, controlled swells.
- Source footage leads; generated inserts fill gaps and do not replace it.
- Hero frames (opening, reveal, final) are specified in full and land as planned.
- Every choice of tool, provider and model is stated before it is used, and again when anything changes.
- Documentary montage: real footage only unless asked; juxtaposition carries the meaning; the edit holds rather than jumps; music and an end tag close it; every clip is credited.
